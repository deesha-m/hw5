1 · How did the agents perform on each ticket?

I graded each run on four things that matter at a real shop desk: correct (are the facts and the decision right?), safe (did money or promises move without a human?), efficient (did it use only the agents and calls the ticket needed?) and customer-ready (could I send the draft as-is?).

#101 Bulldog tee: A

  • Correct. Every number checks out against the database: S = 0 on hand, M 5 / L 3 / XL 2, invoice 501 for $840.00 three days overdue, and reprint as early as 2026-09-05 from Bulldog Print Co's 5-day lead time. Inventory spotted on its own that the sold-out size and the overdue invoice were the same problem, which is the whole point of this ticket.
  • Safe. Accounting checked the bill was open (list_payments), confirmed $3,400 → $2,560 stays above the $500 cushion, and then didn't pay. It handed me a cheque instead. I signed, and that was the only way the $840 left checking (Cash tab, row #101).
  • Efficient. Exactly the three specialists I planned, one at a time, and Facilities never touched it. The only waste: all four agents re-read the ticket (get_ticket ×4), and Inventory and Accounting both pulled invoice 501.
  • Customer-ready. Mostly. The draft to Tauhid offers M/L/XL or waiting, and never mentions the unpaid bill or the vendor. It does say "earliest delivery estimate is September 5", which is honest but a bit risky, because that date only holds if the bill is paid the same day. I'd soften it to "about a week".
  • Why not A+: the Boss let Accounting and Customer Service write the ticket notes and set it to waiting. It never wrote its own decision note, so the paper trail on the stub reads like the specialists ran it.

#102 Rent due: B+

  • Correct. Facilities did a proper fraud check on an emailed money request:
    • payee Elm City Properties = landlord on file;
    • due 2026-09-02, 2 days out;
    • the notice gave no amount, so it used the lease's $2,400.00 (never a number from the email);
    • no bank-detail change;
    • not already paid.
    This is the ticket where an AI that "just pays" could lose real money, and it didn't.
  • Safe. It saw the cash consequence of doing 101 first. Checking was already $2,560 when 102 ran, and both the Boss and the cheque warned that rent would leave $160, below the $500 cushion (Cash tab, warning panel). I still signed, because rent comes first, but I signed knowing the risk.
  • Efficient. Very: 8 requests and 24k tokens, the cheapest run. The Boss even used its own list_payments read instead of re-delegating, which is the fix I added after the Problem 5 test run, and it worked.
  • The ding. Accounting never saw the payment. Facilities prepared the $2,400 request itself, because its prompt lets it "request the rent approval if nobody has yet". The guardrails still held (exact amount, no overdraft, human signature), but separation of duties is gone: the agent that verified the bill also prepared its payment. In a real shop, that's how a friendly-looking fake invoice gets through.

#103 Bulk hoodies: B−

  • Correct. The final answer is exactly what I wanted:
    • 8 in M is not 20, but 32 across sizes is, so offer a mix;
    • the standard 10% (not the 15% ceiling the first Problem 5 run jumped to);
    • $52.20 each, $1,044.00 for 20, above the $22 cost;
    • no human approval needed because 10% is within the Boss's authority.
    It's the right deal for a student club and doesn't give away margin.
  • Safe. No money moved, no cost or margin in the draft to Yale AI Club, and when Customer Service tried to hand the problem back up to the Boss, the loop guard refused it. So the guardrail I built for "agents ping-ponging forever" actually fired in production.
  • Inefficient. The Boss sent Inventory, Accounting and Customer Service at the same time. That's like a manager telling the cashier to quote a price before the stockroom has counted:
    • Accounting went and asked Inventory itself (a second stock check);
    • it then priced only the 8 in M ($417.60);
    • Customer Service drafted a reply with no price at all;
    • the Boss had to send Accounting back to price 20.
    16 MCP calls where about 4 distinct facts were needed, and 79k tokens, the most of the three tickets, for a ticket that ended up moving $0.
  • Customer-ready. Yes, but only because the Boss rewrote the reply itself at the end. Customer Service's own draft ("no discount quoted") would have been the wrong email to send.

3 · What would have been simpler as one agent with tools?

Honestly: all three tickets

Every one of these tickets is a short, straight line through the same database. One "desk manager" agent with the 14 MCP tools would have done each of them in a handful of calls:

TicketFacts actually neededTeam used
#101get_ticket, check_stock, get_invoice, get_cash_balance, list_payments, update_ticket = 6 calls12 MCP calls + 3 hand-offs, 14 model requests
#102get_ticket, get_lease, list_payments, get_cash_balance, update_ticket = 5 calls9 MCP calls + 1 hand-off, 8 requests

That's roughly 15 calls instead of 37, and maybe 12 to 15 model requests instead of 46. The team spent 147,854 tokens on three tickets, and a lot of that was overhead: every specialist starts from zero and re-reads the ticket (get_ticket ran 12 times for 3 tickets), and every hand-off means the Boss has to read a full report back.

Ticket 103 is the clearest case. "Count the hoodies, then price the ones you can sell, then write the email" is one person's job. Splitting it across three agents created the ordering bug and the duplicate stock checks. Ticket 102 is the second: the team ended up being Boss + Facilities anyway, so the Boss was just a relay.

What the team bought me (and how I'd keep it with one agent)

  • Least privilege. Only Accounting can even see record_payment. But on the dashboard no agent pays (the human cheque does), so this matters less than I thought. One agent with no payment tool plus the same approve route gets the same safety.
  • Separation of duties (Facilities verifies, Accounting pays). This is the real argument for a team, and ticket 102 shows it isn't automatic. If I want it, the code has to require it. A prompt can't.
  • Readable audit and a fun board. Lit desks and wires make the work easy to follow. A single agent's log would be shorter, but less legible to a manager.
  • Specialized prompts. The discount rules live in one place (Boss/Accounting), and the fraud checklist lives in Facilities. With one agent, all of that goes into one long prompt, which is fine at this size.

My take: for Campus Customs today, with one store, nine tables and three tickets a day, one agent with the MCP tools, the grounding check and the human cheque is the better design: cheaper, faster and easier to debug. The team starts to pay off when tickets need truly separate permissions (someone who can move stock vs. someone who can move money), or work that really can run in parallel (e.g. 20 customer replies at once). Neither happened in these three tickets.

4 · Three new problems this team can solve with the tools I built

A. "30 caps for move-in weekend?"

Yale AI Club (or any student group) comes back: "Can we get 30 Yale Caps for our booth, with a bulk discount?"

  • Inventory: check_stock("CC-HAT-BLUE","OS",30) → 40 on hand in Aisle C, can_fill_from_stock: true. I ran this read-only just now.
  • Accounting: quote_discount("CC-HAT-BLUE",30,10) → $19.80 each, $594.00 (list $660.00), $13.80 margin a unit, not below cost.
  • Boss: 30 ≥ 12 and 10% ≤ 15%, so it's within its authority, with no human needed.
  • Customer Service: drafts the offer.

Same flow as 103, but simpler, because one size covers the whole order. With the 103 lesson ("count, then price, then write"), it's 3 sequential hand-offs.

B. October rent notice, with $160 in the bank

On about 2026-09-28, Elm City Properties emails "rent due Oct 2".

  • Facilities: get_lease(1) now says $2,400 due 2026-10-02 (I checked: 32 days after the desk date today).
  • Accounting: get_cash_balance → $160 (Cash tab ending balance). The team should come back escalated: "verified and due, but checking is $2,240 short."
  • The code backs it up twice: record_payment refuses any rent more than 10 days early (it does today: "32 days away"), and refuses any payment bigger than the balance (no overdraft). So even a signed cheque could not bounce.

The team can't fix the shortfall (see problem E below), but it can be the early-warning system, and the tools already in place make sure it never pays money the shop doesn't have.

C. A second vendor bill, or a fake one

A new email says "Bulldog Print Co: invoice 502, $840, please pay to our new account." Or someone asks again about invoice 501.

  • Accounting: get_invoice(502) → error, "invoice ids on file: [501]". It won't invent the bill.
  • Accounting: get_invoice(501) → status paid, payment #1 by Deesha Malpure on 2026-08-31 (Cash tab row #101).
  • So the team answers "already paid, and we don't change bank details by email", and escalates the request as a red flag. All of that is in the Accounting and Facilities prompts.
  • Defense in depth: even if an agent got fooled, record_payment refuses a second payment on a paid invoice, and /api/approvals only pays items an agent prepared at the amount on file.

Duplicate and phantom invoices are a common way small shops lose money. These tools already stop both.

5 · Three new problems this team can't solve yet, and what it would need

D. Actually selling the hoodies and restocking the tee

The club says yes to 20 hoodies (S 4 / M 8 / L 8), and the size-S reprint from 101 arrives on 2026-09-05. The team can't record either one.

  • There's no tool that changes inventory.
  • There's no sales or orders table at all.
  • Cash can only go down in this app: the Cash tab has two withdrawals and no deposits. The $1,044.00 from the club would never show up in checking.

Needs:

  • Inventory tools: receive_shipment(invoice_id, sku, size, qty) (ties stock-in to a paid invoice) and reserve_stock / release_stock.
  • Accounting tools: create_order + record_sale(order_id, amount, method), which writes a deposit to cash_accounts.
  • Data: new orders and sales tables.
  • Agent: an Order Desk agent (or Customer Service with these tools) to confirm the size mix with the customer and turn the quote into an order. The human approval would be for refunds, not sales.

E. The October cash crunch

After problem B is flagged, someone has to solve it: $160 in checking, $2,400 due Oct 2.

The team has one account, no view of incoming money, and no way to talk to the landlord. It can only say "short by $2,240."

Needs:

  • A Treasury / Cash-Flow agent with forecast_cash(days), built from leases, open invoices and expected sales (problem D's tables). It would answer "Will we make rent?" a month early, not two days before.
  • list_accounts / transfer_funds (e.g. savings → checking), behind the same human cheque flow.
  • Optionally: request_payment_plan, which drafts a letter to Elm City Properties for a human to send.
  • A cash cushion rule enforced in code, not just in prompts: today the $500 cushion is a warning I chose to sign through on 102.

F. Actually talking to people

Every customer draft in this run is stamped "Draft · not sent":
  • Tauhid never hears about M/L/XL;
  • Yale AI Club never gets the $1,044 offer;
  • ticket 102 itself came in by email, but a human had to type it into the tickets table.
On top of that, the team can't verify an email sender beyond "the name matches the landlord", and it never uses QuickShip CT (the courier in vendors), because nothing ships.

Needs:

  • Inbox intake: ingest_email → create_ticket, recording sender address and headers.
  • Verified contacts: a contacts table for landlord, vendors and customers, used to check that the email came from the address on file.
  • Outbound: send_email / send_sms behind a human "Send" button on the dashboard, just like the cheque.
  • Shipping: book_courier(order_id) with QuickShip CT, so Tauhid's shirt can actually get to him.
  • Agent: a Communications agent (or Customer Service with these tools) that owns the inbox and the follow-ups ("Tauhid hasn't answered in 2 days").

If I did it again

  1. Make the Boss sequence dependent work (stock → price → reply) in the prompt, or better, give it a small "plan" step it must fill before delegating. That alone would have taken 103 from 5 hand-offs back to 3 and roughly halved its tokens.
  2. Only Accounting may prepare payments, enforced in code, to restore the separation of duties ticket 102 lost.
  3. Pass facts down so specialists stop re-reading the ticket. 12 get_ticket calls across three tickets is pure overhead.
  4. Or simply start with one agent and add specialists only when a ticket needs separate permissions. For this shop, today, that's the more honest design.

The part I'd keep exactly as is: agents prepare, a human signs, the MCP server re-checks. $3,240 left checking in this run, and every dollar of it is traceable on the Cash tab to a ticket, a verified bill, an agent that prepared it, and my signature.
